The story

A letter drops into the slot of a forgotten mailbox. It is addressed to the giant tortoise who carries a city on her back.

You are Tilo, a small clockwork mailbox who hops everywhere and has not forgotten his duty. The rusty city on the Tortoise’s shell has stood still for years: the Ballast Guild shut the Post Office, melted down the stamps and drove in anchors so nothing would ever move again.

Someone has to deliver the last letter. Tilo is all that’s left.

What makes it different

Look, poke, think. Above all, stamp things.

A point-and-click adventure for looking, poking and thinking. Pick things up, combine them, help the robots and odd creatures you meet, and solve hands-on mechanical puzzles up close.

01

Not one word

The story is told with drawings, gestures and sound. Characters ask for what they want in pictogram bubbles, and Tilo thinks in pictures.

02

Stamps are your words

Received, Urgent, Fragile… Each stamp is an idea the world understands: stamp Urgent on a label and the queue steps aside.

03

A thief for a friend

Spark, a copper mouse with a knack for stealing, becomes your companion and squeezes in where Tilo can’t fit.

04

Choices with no wrong answer

Some problems have three good solutions. What you pick goes on your route sheet and decides which of three endings you’ll see.

Features

  • Built for touch: play everything with one finger.
  • About 4 hours: a prologue and six chapters.
  • Over 40 puzzles per playthrough: items, stamps, your companion and mechanical close-ups.
  • Three different endings, shaped by your choices.
  • 15 lost letters to find. Deliver them and see what changes.
  • Picture hints in two steps whenever you’re stuck.
  • No dead ends, no timers: play at your own pace.
  • Autosave and three save slots.

Accessibility

  • A “reveal” button shows everything you can touch.
  • Dragging is optional: tap the item, then tap where it goes.
  • Reduced motion and softened lightning options.
  • Interface scaling up to 150%.
  • Optional pictogram subtitles in English and Spanish.
  • Nothing is told through sound alone.
  • Wordless guides teach the gestures, and you can turn them off.
